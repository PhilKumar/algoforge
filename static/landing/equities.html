Chapter I · The Banyan

Bombay, 1875.

A handful of cotton and share brokers met each afternoon in the shade of a banyan tree, because no one had yet built them a room. They agreed prices standing up, on nothing but reputation. Asia's oldest stock exchange grew out of that patch of shade.

Chapter II · The Ring

For a century you could hear the market.

The tree became a floor, and the floor became a roar — hundreds of men in a pit, quoting with their hands because a voice could not carry. You knew a panic had started before you knew what it was about.

In 1995 the ring was switched off. The shouting moved into the wire, and the loudest market in Asia went completely silent in a single stroke.

Chapter III · The Screen

The crowd didn't leave. It just stopped shouting.

Every bid that used to be a raised hand is now a row in a book, and the fear and greed that moved the pit still print the same shapes on the chart. Nothing about the crowd changed — only the volume.

Chapter IV · The Bell

This market closes. That changes everything.

Crypto never stops, so a position can always be managed. Here the session ends at half past three and the next price you see is tomorrow's open — which may be nowhere near yesterday's close. A desk built for equities has to respect the bell, not ignore it.

What the desk actually does
The Mother

A rally runs, then one candle reaches high and gives more than half of it back as an upper wick. That rejection is the candle everything else is measured from — and it is confirmed only at its own close, never in hindsight.

The Ladder

One entry is a guess. Rungs are placed down from the mother, each armed only below the previous round's low, so a fall that keeps falling is bought deeper rather than averaged blindly.

The Contract

Monthly options only, 15 to 45 days out. Weeklies die before a thesis can breathe; a monthly gives the trade the runway it needed all along.

Where this actually is

Paper first. Still proving.

The equities desk runs the same geometry as the crypto side and replays it across roughly 22 months of history in the Test Bench. What it does not have yet is a live track record worth quoting — the amounts traded so far prove the plumbing, not the edge.

So there are no performance figures on this page. When there is a record honest enough to show, it will appear here with its losses attached.

Between the bells

Six hours, fifteen minutes.
Then the market goes home.

Everything this desk does has to happen inside that window — armed before the open, honest about the gap, squared off before the bell.
